Three cases you can test

We ran these fictional scenarios in Polish in Codex. Each example includes an English translation of the prompt, a summary of the response, and an official source. Copy a prompt to try it yourself.

Administrative procedure

Appealing a housing benefit refusal

The appeal deadline in the example was 23 November 2026. Codex explained why it shifted from Saturday, flagged that ordinary email was ineffective for filing the appeal, and drafted the appeal.

Polish Code of Administrative Procedure
Show the prompt 
$prawnik-pl It is 19 November 2026. On 3 November the mayor refused my housing benefit application. I picked up the decision in person at the post office on Saturday, 7 November. The decision says I have 14 days from service to appeal to the Local Government Appeals Board (SKO) through the mayor. I suspect the office counted one payment twice. I have a certificate from my employer and a copy of my application.

Give me the last day to appeal, show how you calculated it, and check whether I can file by ordinary email. Prepare a short appeal with placeholders for my details and attachments.

Consumer rights

Getting a refund for a leaking espresso machine

The distance-sale withdrawal deadline in the example was 20 November 2026. Codex compared withdrawal, a statutory complaint to the seller, and a guarantee claim, then drafted a message to the seller.

Polish Consumer Rights Act
Show the prompt 
$prawnik-pl It is 19 November 2026. I bought an espresso machine as a consumer from a Polish online store for PLN 1,249. I received it on 6 November. On the third use, water started leaking from the casing, so I unplugged it. On 16 November I wrote to the store: “The espresso machine is leaking. Please tell me how to report the fault.” The next day the store directed me to the manufacturer’s service department. I have proof of purchase and a video of the leak.

I want my money back. Compare my options, say whether I need to act today, and draft a message to the seller. Explain which requests I can make immediately and which depend on other circumstances.

Residential tenancy

Recovering a deposit after moving out

The deposit was due on 30 October 2026 in the example. Codex calculated four possible balances (PLN 2,000, 1,500, 500, or 0), identified supporting evidence, and drafted a demand for payment.

Polish Tenant Protection Act
Show the prompt 
$prawnik-pl It is 19 November 2026. I rented a flat on a standard lease from 1 October 2024 to 30 September 2026. Monthly rent was PLN 2,500 and the deposit was PLN 3,000. I returned the keys on 30 September. The signed handover report records “minor signs of wear on the walls” and the electricity meter reading. I had no rent arrears.

On 7 November the landlord returned PLN 1,000. In an email, the landlord said PLN 1,500 was deducted for painting and PLN 500 for electricity. No bills were attached. I have photos from the start and end of the tenancy. How much can I claim depending on which deductions the landlord can justify? Check the deposit deadline, identify what evidence I need, and draft a demand for payment.

About the research project

Prawnik PL was created in preparation for an academic publication. The initial tests covered three fictional cases. A broader assessment of response quality and an independent review of the results are part of the ongoing research.

The project was co-authored by Dr. habil. Denis Solodov, associate professor at the University of Warmia and Mazury in Olsztyn (ORCID), and Dr. Ilia Solodov, a lawyer and digital forensics specialist.

Help test the beta

We invite lawyers, researchers, law students, and AI users to test the skill. Include the skill version, model version, anonymised prompt, and the relevant excerpt from the response. For legal errors, include the official source and the date for which the rule should be checked.
